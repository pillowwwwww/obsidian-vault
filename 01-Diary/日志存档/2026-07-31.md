## Tracking

Thank you for synthesizing the reviewers’ concerns. Most of the requested robustness axes were already examined in the submitted main paper and appendix, including component and gate ablations, bypass-strength sensitivity, rank and participation robustness, subgroup diagnostics, and runtime analysis. Our rebuttal consolidates these existing results, adds targeted analyses of the recorded trajectories and client endpoints, and provides a small number of focused extensions where the reviewers requested longer or broader evaluation.

1. The submitted paper already provides component and gate ablations (Table 4 and Appendix Table 6), as well as the same eight-value $\eta$ sweep for every base aggregator (Figure 6 and Appendix Table 10), which shows broad response regions rather than narrow optima. It also examines robustness across LoRA ranks, participation rates, and client subgroups (Appendix Tables 7–9).
   In the rebuttal, we keep the algorithm and main protocol fixed while further testing a shared $\eta=10$ across aggregators and matched seeds, varying the spectral boundary and numerical safeguard, and repeating the comparison under different participation schedules. The results remain stable across these settings, showing that FedReCall does not rely on a precise boundary, numerical cutoff, or aggregator-specific tuning.

2. 
 The submitted motivation diagnostics already show structured attenuation of weak directions during federated training. In the rebuttal, we formally separate ordinary heterogeneous-update attenuation from the additional factor-wise channel:

\[ X_{\\mathrm{exact}}-X_{\\mathrm{fw}} =s\\sum_jp_j(B_j-\\bar B)(A_j-\\bar A). \]

 We derive its exact harmful condition and magnitude bound, together with sufficient spectral-separation conditions under which persistent client-private components occupy weak singular positions while remaining distinguishable from sampling noise. A zero-effective-conflict control using actual trained adapters isolates the factor-wise channel while holding the effective updates and evaluation data fixed. Complementary restoration, removal, matched-random, peer, and sign-reversal controls further show that the affected weak directions contain owner-specific functional information rather than arbitrary low-rank noise.
 
 Detailed derivations and control results are provided in our responses to Reviewer TQRP (W1, factor-wise causal separation) and Reviewer yFcC (W1, formal conditions and signal-versus-noise controls). Together, these results distinguish general FL conflict from the additional factor-wise amplification and establish when the recalled weak directions represent useful client-specific signal.
