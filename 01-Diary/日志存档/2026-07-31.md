The results remain stable across these settings, showing that FedReCall does not rely on a precise boundary, numerical cutoff, or aggregator-specific tuning.

 The submitted motivation diagnostics already show structured attenuation of weak directions during federated training. In the rebuttal, we formally separate ordinary heterogeneous-update attenuation from the additional factor-wise channel:

\[ X_{\\mathrm{exact}}-X_{\\mathrm{fw}} =s\\sum_jp_j(B_j-\\bar B)(A_j-\\bar A). \]

 We derive its exact harmful condition and magnitude bound, together with sufficient spectral-separation conditions under which persistent client-private components occupy weak singular positions while remaining distinguishable from sampling noise. A zero-effective-conflict control using actual trained adapters isolates the factor-wise channel while holding the effective updates and evaluation data fixed. Complementary restoration, removal, matched-random, peer, and sign-reversal controls further show that the affected weak directions contain owner-specific functional information rather than arbitrary low-rank noise.
 
Detailed derivations and control results are provided in our responses to Reviewer TQRP (W1, factor-wise causal separation) and Reviewer yFcC (W1, formal conditions and signal-versus-noise controls). Together, the theoretical characterization and controlled experiments establish the paper’s intended distinction: heterogeneous FL creates baseline directional attenuation, while factor-wise LoRA aggregation introduces a separate and measurable amplification channel. They further show that, under the stated spectral conditions, the weak directions affected by this channel carry owner-specific functional information rather than matched low-rank noise.

**4.** The limited rank-16 result and the early negative result at 30% participation arise from candidate-pool capacity and training budget, respectively. As detailed in our response to Reviewer 415c (Q3/Q4/W2), controlled experiments verify both explanations. At rank 16, the fixed relative boundary exposes too few candidate directions, while a pre-specified expanded-pool diagnostic recovers clear gains, confirming that useful directions remain available. At 30% participation, three-seed matched runs extended to 50 rounds show that the initial loss disadvantage disappears and becomes a stable positive gain. Across participation rates, the measured dilution gap, active cache rank, and correction energy decrease as cache age shortens, confirming reduced recovery headroom rather than stale-cache harm.

We further evaluate a different instruction dataset, a 7B model, client populations up to 200, and matched federations with different numbers of task types, as detailed in our response to Reviewer 415c (W1). FedReCall remains effective across the evaluated datasets, model scales, population sizes, and heterogeneity levels. The larger gains under greater task heterogeneity coincide with higher update dispersion and a larger dilution gap, indicating that FedReCall is most beneficial when more client-specific directional information is lost during aggregation.
